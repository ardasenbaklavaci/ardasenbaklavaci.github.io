Thirteen years after Los Santos, Rockstar actually shipped it. Grand Theft Auto VI arrived worldwide on **19 November 2026** for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, and the launch has been followed by the two things every modern release brings: a wave of patch notes and a wave of "where is the PC version" questions.

This post is a status check rather than a review. I have not finished the story, so I am not going to pretend I know how it ends. What I can do is put the confirmed facts in one place: what shipped, what Rockstar has patched so far, what the update cadence looks like, and what is still officially unannounced.

## Where the launch stands

The short version: it is out, it is enormous, and it is single-player only.

| Item | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Release date | 19 November 2026 |
| Platforms | PlayStation 5 (PS5 Pro Enhanced), Xbox Series X\|S |
| Editions | Standard $79.99, Ultimate $99.99 |
| Install size | Up to 212 GB, plus updates |
| Preload | Digital from 10 November, retail code-in-box from 12 November |
| Modes | Story mode only, no microtransactions at launch |
| PC version | Not announced |
| Online mode | Not announced |

A few details worth knowing if you have not bought it yet. The physical release is a **code in a box**, not a disc, so the console still downloads most of the game. Preload started on 10 November, which is why so many people were already playing at midnight local time on launch day. And the **Vintage Vice City Pack** plus a free month of GTA+ were pre-order bonuses that ended on launch day itself, so that particular window is closed.

On the business side, Take-Two's own numbers are hard to read without raising an eyebrow. The company reported **$3.79 billion in retail sales on day one** and **32.5 million copies sold through launch**, both presented as records. Third-party trackers that measure pre-orders had lower figures, which is normal: publishers count sell-in to retail and digital entitlements, trackers count what they can see. Treat the exact numbers as marketing until Take-Two's next earnings call, and treat the scale as real either way.

The streaming side is easier to verify. According to Streams Charts data, the launch peaked at **7.6 million concurrent viewers** across Twitch, YouTube and Kick, making it the biggest category Twitch has ever hosted.

## The first patch is already out

Rockstar began rolling out **update 01.002.002** on **23 November**, four days after launch. On PS5 it weighs about **604 MB**; on Xbox (version 1.0.2.2) it is roughly **1.6 GB**. The PS5 rollout went out in waves over about six hours, so a friend getting it before you is not a sign that something is wrong with your console.

This is the first patch Rockstar actually explained. The two updates before launch shipped with no notes at all.

| Version | Date | PS5 size | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 01.002.000 | 13 November 2026 | ~1.07 GB | Pre-launch, no notes |
| 01.002.001 | 16 November 2026 | ~602 MB | Pre-launch, no notes |
| 01.002.002 | 23 November 2026 | ~604 MB | 7 documented fixes |

The full list from Rockstar Support, under "General / Miscellaneous":

1. Fixed several issues that caused the game to crash.
2. Fixed an issue that resulted in the weather not changing for extended periods.
3. Fixed an issue that resulted in the game not autosaving correctly.
4. Fixed phone calls not triggering correctly, including calls that progressed the story or unlocked missions.
5. Fixed players being unable to lose their Wanted Level.
6. Fixed the deadlift bar in the gym being unusable.
7. Fixed partners not handing over bags so they could be sold to fences.

Two of those are more serious than they look. The **autosave bug** convinced a lot of people that the game had no autosave at all, and the **phone call bug** could leave a player standing in the world with nothing to do, because several story beats arrive as a call. The Wanted Level fix matters too: a star rating you cannot clear turns a small mistake into a soft lock.

The weather fix is the funny one. Plenty of players assumed the permanently sunny sky was an artistic decision. It was a bug.

## What about the crashes?

In the first weekend, players reported crashes to the dashboard on PS5 with error **CE-108255-1**, and similar hard crashes on Xbox Series X. Rockstar's notes mention "several crash fixes" but do not name that error code, so it is not clear whether every case is covered.

If you are still crashing after 01.002.002, the usual console checklist actually helps here:

- **Reboot the console properly** (Rest Mode is not a reboot) and let the patch finish installing before launching.
- **Clear the console cache**: on PS5, safe mode option 5 clears the cache without touching saves.
- **Free up storage.** A 212 GB game on a nearly full drive is a bad combination; leave a few dozen gigabytes of headroom.
- **Check the game's save data integrity** if you crash in the same mission repeatedly, and report it through Rockstar Support with the mission name, since a reproducible crash is the kind they can actually patch.

Sony's own error page for CE-108255-1 is generic advice, because the code means "the application crashed", not "the hardware is broken". Do not factory reset your console over it.

## Updates are the new normal

Here is the part that matters for the next twelve months. A game this size does not ship finished, and Rockstar has always treated launch as the start of a long support cycle rather than the end of one.

What we can reasonably expect, based on how the studio handled GTA V and Red Dead Redemption 2:

- **Small, frequent hotfixes** in the first month, like the one above. Sizes in the hundreds of megabytes, mostly stability and progression fixes.
- **Quality-of-life patches** after that: UI complaints, mission checkpoints, performance modes, accessibility options. These are the ones that follow the loudest community feedback.
- **Content updates** later. For GTA V that meant new vehicles, activities and eventually a new version of the game for newer consoles. Nothing has been announced for GTA VI yet.

The page to bookmark is Rockstar Support's **"Grand Theft Auto VI Launch Updates (PS5 / XBOX Series X|S)"** article, which is where notes appear first. The Newswire is where anything bigger gets announced.

One practical tip: turn on automatic updates and leave the console in Rest Mode overnight. A six-hour staged rollout is a lot less annoying when it happens while you are asleep.

## The PC version is still not announced

This is the question I get asked most, and the answer has not changed since launch day: **there is no PC version, no Steam page, no Epic listing, and no published system requirements.**

Every official document, including Take-Two's 9 November earnings release and Rockstar's own preload post, lists only PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S.

There is exactly one credible report pointing at a date. In October 2026, GamesBeat cited four sources familiar with Rockstar's plans saying the studio was targeting **Q1 2027** for PC. That would be a dramatically shorter console-to-PC gap than GTA V's (September 2013 to April 2015), and it fits the fact that the console versions were built with current-gen hardware in mind from the start.

It is still a report, not an announcement. Do not buy a GPU for a game with no store page.

If you want to play now on a PC monitor, your realistic options are Remote Play from a PS5, or Xbox Cloud Gaming, which lists GTA VI for Xbox consoles rather than PC. Neither is the same thing as a native port.

## Online is not here either

Both console store pages describe Grand Theft Auto VI as "a single-player experience", the PS5 listing says "1 player", and none of the game's 55 trophies are for online play. Rockstar has not named, dated or described a multiplayer mode.

The reporting points at **2027**. Bloomberg's Jason Schreier said in October that multiplayer was expected in 2027, and a Bloomberg Originals video framed it as the game's "real long-term engine" arriving about a year after the story mode. Twitch CEO Dan Clancy said in September that he expected a bigger viewership bump "the following year when multiplayer launches" — that is his expectation, not a Rockstar date.

What you will see repeated elsewhere is that online is coming "about a month after launch". There is no Rockstar source behind that; it traces back to an April leaker claim. Treat every date you see as speculation until it is on the Newswire.

In the meantime, **GTA Online for GTA V is still running** and still getting weekly content, and **GTA+** now bundles some single-player bonuses for GTA VI alongside its usual GTA Online perks. If you were planning to subscribe for multiplayer, wait.

## What to watch next

Three things will tell us where this is going:

1. **The next patch.** If it is another small stability update within two weeks, Rockstar is in bug-fix mode. If it starts touching balance, missions or performance modes, that is the quality-of-life phase.
2. **Support page updates.** A new article category for anything other than "Story Mode Play" would be the first real hint of an online component.
3. **Take-Two's next earnings call.** That is where a PC or multiplayer window would realistically be mentioned first, because shareholders ask.

GTA VI launched in a remarkably clean state for a game of this scope, and the first patch fixed actual progression blockers rather than cosmetics. That is a good sign. The bigger question — whether the game grows the way GTA V did — will be answered by patches, not by launch week.
