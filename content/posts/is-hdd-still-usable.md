But still today, there are still some people use it fore some reasons we will explain at this post.</p><p></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/a/AVvXsEj7OQV0UcsfJ-CSdDnimgONe8X0tyEJt-BziPGFiKqmonKuy38LIShLiYmi45gSWHFCkxzvZg8Iot5BfVXdC3cEcAgRZDxvV8y-0Hwo5m_LwZbh-9fu9PdZmC21aBUqxkk1PzGAaNz2BYOiiuOdpwcgH6XgAy8_PyFybsVuLFbZAH9-4ulPQ6jCTgbucPk" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img alt="" data-original-height="1000" data-original-width="1000" height="364" src="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/a/AVvXsEj7OQV0UcsfJ-CSdDnimgONe8X0tyEJt-BziPGFiKqmonKuy38LIShLiYmi45gSWHFCkxzvZg8Iot5BfVXdC3cEcAgRZDxvV8y-0Hwo5m_LwZbh-9fu9PdZmC21aBUqxkk1PzGAaNz2BYOiiuOdpwcgH6XgAy8_PyFybsVuLFbZAH9-4ulPQ6jCTgbucPk" width="364" /></a></div><br /><p></p>

<p>If you are a gamer on a budget, storing/managing large libraries or work on reviving an old system, using HDD is still viable solution for this system with right expectation... Also can be better with some optimizations...</p>

<p>In this post we will talk about HDD's today's situation and its comparisons with SSD, according to the performance and affordability issues, and how we can get the best possible experience from a setup with HDD, what we will expect in terms of performance and price.</p><p>Since almost every PC users and producers have switched to NVME SSD's, HDD have became a side/background storage device instead of main OS/Program device... Some of hosting companies use HDD for light hosting. Also you can boot light, old or indie games with small size from HDD without any lags...</p>

<h2>HDD vs SSD: Why SSDs Became the Standard</h2>

<p>SSD (Solid State Drives) are researched and developed to go beyond the speed/latency limits of Hard Disk Drives have. HDD's do work based on spinning magnetic disk and mechanical moving parts which have latency multiple times slower and delayed with respect to SSD's. But HDD's do have larger capacities per price rate. HDD has longer boot time, slower read/write speed and has risk of mechanical failure especially can happen in case of portable devices like laptop...</p><p></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/a/AVvXsEhFsrb6el4C_ZN502-cInLzoDLbmoTLFR8Zh5FUw5Sczfjm73Q2WI_mVplXiDTEMo0GARTeF6UqQVZZ88m2fESFvtEhTOBBl9R-ozQEwcR7Gghr1rdNvEN_9k5GhEoNcFWTwivDsi8TtUd1FLa1k9DQmYAxwBE4hk2OBejwL_FdCbLOatqAupWncjjD4as" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img alt="" data-original-height="450" data-original-width="1200" height="232" src="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/a/AVvXsEhFsrb6el4C_ZN502-cInLzoDLbmoTLFR8Zh5FUw5Sczfjm73Q2WI_mVplXiDTEMo0GARTeF6UqQVZZ88m2fESFvtEhTOBBl9R-ozQEwcR7Gghr1rdNvEN_9k5GhEoNcFWTwivDsi8TtUd1FLa1k9DQmYAxwBE4hk2OBejwL_FdCbLOatqAupWncjjD4as=w619-h232" width="619" /></a></div><br /><p></p>

<p>SSD's use flash memory chips without no any parts (as in HDD we have rotating disks and rotating readers...)... SSD's were invented to deliver faster data read/write. It is also better energy efficient for our computing needs for both daily tasks and other entertaining or productive things that need system performance, disk write/read operations...</p>

<p>By the time, SSD's became the a bit expensive but valuable solution for speeding up loading time of OS/apps, totally increasing the overall system performance and visual experience smoothness... Also we know that SSD's are mainly developed to increase the speed of Laptops. As we know laptops do have limited CPU/Ram and Bus speeds because of power consumption needs... SSD's do relax the I/O operations in laptops.&nbsp;</p>

<h2>Statistical Comparison: HDD vs SSD vs NVMe</h2>

<h3>What is an HDD?</h3>

<p>HDD is cheap and slow solution. But more safe...</p>

<p>As we talk for security of data: Even mechanism, chips etc... get broken on HDD, you can save data in spinning platter (disc). This makes the HDD as easier for data recovery in case of mechanical or chip failure of the drive.</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Speed:</strong> ~80–160 MB/s (slowest)</li>
<li><strong>Technology:</strong> Mechanical parts (spinning platter + moving head)</li>
<li><strong>Price:</strong> Cheapest per GB</li>
<li><strong>Lifespan:</strong> Good, but prone to mechanical failure over time</li>
<li><strong>Best For:</strong>
<ul>
<li>Mass storage</li>
<li>Backup systems and manually storing Backup Data's, Backup, Database Backup Sequental Image Files...</li>
<li>Movie/Music/Image media libraries&nbsp;</li>
<li>Light Budget PCs</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<hr />

<h3>What is an SSD? (SATA SSD)</h3>

<p><strong>SSD (Solid State Drive)</strong> uses flash memory chips (usually SATA-based but has NAND too) and has no mechanical moving parts. It’s significantly faster than HDD but can be less reliable...</p><p></p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/a/AVvXsEgLLu_UGUmkOyipuMDu05B-cEpDu5F6TlchGwEiQOgKRwaMwUvYfbsSVtEqRt7rlvQvwg3-35ufGEMLXPAV-slCWNWQHB96SNwoRXgbBEPIXFxXV8cIyi3wtZN6WkfSVae-t1ZGvtbWKwvqdJywCEvzUWIhJd8gKAtQYL_4uD38He455BUIsUWholgnAMw" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img alt="" data-original-height="375" data-original-width="375" height="240" src="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/a/AVvXsEgLLu_UGUmkOyipuMDu05B-cEpDu5F6TlchGwEiQOgKRwaMwUvYfbsSVtEqRt7rlvQvwg3-35ufGEMLXPAV-slCWNWQHB96SNwoRXgbBEPIXFxXV8cIyi3wtZN6WkfSVae-t1ZGvtbWKwvqdJywCEvzUWIhJd8gKAtQYL_4uD38He455BUIsUWholgnAMw" width="240" /></a></div><br /><br /><p></p>

<p>It is hard, maybe almost near impossible, expensive to save data from a dead ssd, making it unsafe to save data's, according to HDD. Also SSD's are more sensitive to Statical Electric which can cause failure more in SSD's. Statical Electric can cause the passing electric inside circuits to short...</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Speed:</strong> ~500–550 MB/s (SATA SSD). <strong>SATA SSDs</strong> use <strong>NAND flash memory</strong> (just like other SSDs) but uses <strong>SATA III interface</strong> for data transfer, which has a <strong>maximum speed of 6 Gbps</strong> (~550 MB/s in real-world use). It may be limited speed because of <strong>SATA III interface</strong>. Some low GB Sata SSD's have lower write speeds down to 150 MB/s... Even you have the Fastest chip, SATA interface will limit your speed. It is the same interface as latest Hard Drives.&nbsp;</li>

<li><strong>Technology:</strong> NAND Flash via SATA interface same as Modern Hard Drives...</li>

<li><strong>Price:</strong> High Per GB, since Sata SSD's are demanding low, a bit cheaper that NVME drives...</li>

<li><strong>Lifespan:</strong> Normally longer than HDD, but can suddenly fail because of short cut...</li>

<li><strong>Best For:</strong>
<ul>
<li>OS boot device</li>
<li>Everyday tasks</li>
<li>Mid to high level gaming (500 - 550mb/s read speed can be insufficient for heavy model and RTX Games)</li>
<li>Laptops and desktops</li>
</ul>
</li>
</ul>

<h3>What is NVMe SSD?</h3><div><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><a href="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/a/AVvXsEjerSHU4k1wEl5PkQsBMd65CCOFuXQ21vlL4tWS6h822Dv2vAOGlUE4FEzPD5qU1BXZMn6-nUAxg14rgwGq7SFHImkBFIP2qRTresrsOEjj372uITv_h-c9BM8P5adWghdravpumXaKk4bpKU-tM8WkyvVjSahCkTLJc567MaSTZTQlrwSo5qxWJLHaQsM" style="margin-left: 1em; margin-right: 1em;"><img alt="" data-original-height="430" data-original-width="287" height="346" src="https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/a/AVvXsEjerSHU4k1wEl5PkQsBMd65CCOFuXQ21vlL4tWS6h822Dv2vAOGlUE4FEzPD5qU1BXZMn6-nUAxg14rgwGq7SFHImkBFIP2qRTresrsOEjj372uITv_h-c9BM8P5adWghdravpumXaKk4bpKU-tM8WkyvVjSahCkTLJc567MaSTZTQlrwSo5qxWJLHaQsM=w231-h346" width="231" /></a></div><br /><br /></div>

<p><strong>NVMe (Non-Volatile Memory Express)</strong> is the newest type of SSD that has direct connection between motherboard and SSD. By the direct connection through PCIe slot, it bypasses the SATA interface's bandwith limitation. Having almost 10-15 times faster transfer speed and lower delay than SATA SSD.&nbsp;</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Speed:</strong> 3,000–7,000+ MB/s (fastest SSD solution). Speeds may change according to the Pci-e version (3.0-5.0)</li>
<li><strong>Technology:</strong> NAND Flash via higher speed and low latency PCIe interface connected directly to the motherboard</li>
<li><strong>Price:</strong> Higher per GB (prices are fluctiating according to the demand/production)</li>
<li><strong>Lifespan:</strong> Similar to SATA SSDs, but varies by quality, brand and type of Flash Chip used...</li>
<li><strong>Best For:</strong>
</li><ul>
<li>High end gaming</li>
<li>Video editing, 3D rendering that needs to write the rendered image models to the disk or read...</li>
<li>Multitasking power users</li>
<li>Modern laptops and gaming rigs</li>
</ul>

</ul><div><h3>SSD Timeline</h3><ul><li><strong>Early 1990s:</strong> SSD and its other like technologies used in military and enterprise.</li><li><strong>Mid–2000s:</strong> First PC style consumer SSDs (very expensive, low capacity) started using.</li><li><strong>2010s onward:</strong> Production of SSD's do incerase and price starts dropping to the daily budgets, mainstream adoption in PCs and laptops have increased.</li><li><strong>Today (2025):</strong> SSD's are now the standard for PC's. NVME drives do dominate the market because of its ultra fast speeds. SATA SSD's are still developing but you get much faster NVME drives for almost same price today...&nbsp;</li></ul></div><div><br /></div>

<h2>Final Thoughts: Is HDD Still Worth It in 2025?</h2>

<p>HDD's are no longer the best option for modern operating systems and high-end gaming, but they still have important uses in 2025.&nbsp;</p><p>For extreme budget/cheap systems, since the performance is ignored, the HDD can be used. But giving just a little budget for small ssd can be good... Also for backups, NAS systems, storages, and systems that used as a GIT, HDD can be the solution with cheap and large solution.&nbsp;</p>

<p>However, SSD and NVMe drives provide much better performance overally for gaming. Like loading times, responsiveness and smoother gaming experience. Today, the most balanced setup for many users is using an SSD or NVMe drive for both operating system, productivity, and gaming... Also keeping an HDD for large file storage, backup and archives.&nbsp;</p><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><br /></div><br /><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><br /></div><br /><div class="separator" style="clear: both; text-align: center;"><br /></div><br />
