<!--wp:heading-->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">🔌 What Does a PSU Actually Do?</h2>
<!--/wp:heading-->

<!--wp:paragraph-->
<p>PSU is the power supply unit of your PC... It gets alternative current electricity from your wall outlet which is comes from your City, converts it into the DC and feeds electric to power your motherboard, feeding the CPU, GPU, Hard Drives, Fans etc... Any part that uses electricity current that it needs... It’s also responsible for voltage regulation and having a stable pc running state by providing voltage regulation. A bad PSU has potential of causing all kinds of weird issues like random shutdowns, data corruption, or having overvoltage to the components, frying them...</p>
<!--/wp:paragraph-->

<!--wp:separator-->
<hr class="wp-block-separator has-alpha-channel-opacity" />
<!--/wp:separator-->

<!--wp:heading-->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">💭 When You <em>Can</em> Reuse an Old PSU</h2>
<!--/wp:heading-->

<!--wp:heading {"level":3}-->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">✅ 1. It’s Not <em>Too</em> Old and Brand Quality</h3>
<!--/wp:heading-->

<!--wp:paragraph-->
<p>If your PSU is less than 5 years old and was a quality brand (Corsair, Seasonic, EVGA, etc.), it has high chance of stable and safe use...</p>
<!--/wp:paragraph-->

<!--wp:heading {"level":3}-->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">✅ 2. It Serves the right ATX Version For your Build</h3><div>Before mounting the old PSU to the new system, you need to check if it is eligible for the new system.&nbsp;</div><div>You need to make sure that the old PSU has the correct ATX Version requirement of the Motherboard and Gpu... Otherwise it will not work or even it will be not able to connected to the parts...&nbsp;</div>

<h3 class="wp-block-heading">✅ 3. It Has Enough Wattage</h3>
<!--/wp:heading-->

<!--wp:paragraph-->
<p>By the time passing, CPU's and GPU's use more power because of increased core frequencies, speeds and voltages... Shortly the TDP consumption increases especially for GPU and CPU. You need to have a wattage that is an strict "Overkill" for your whole parts inside your PC. For a modern gaming pc build, 650W–850W is often the sweet spot today...</p>
<!--/wp:paragraph-->

<!--wp:paragraph-->
<p>Let have a sample gaming pc and its power consumptions of each part:</p>
<!--/wp:paragraph-->

<!--wp:list-->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!--wp:list-item-->
<li><strong>CPU</strong>: 125W</li>
<!--/wp:list-item-->

<!--wp:list-item-->
<li><p><strong>GPU</strong>: 350W</p></li>
<!--/wp:list-item-->

<!--wp:list-item-->
<li><p><strong>Motherboard</strong>: 60W</p></li>
<!--/wp:list-item-->

<!--wp:list-item-->
<li><p><strong>RAM (4x8GB)</strong>: 20W</p></li>
<!--/wp:list-item-->

<!--wp:list-item-->
<li><p><strong>Storage</strong>: SSD (5W) + HDD (8W) = 13W</p></li>
<!--/wp:list-item-->

<!--wp:list-item-->
<li><p><strong>Cooling Fans</strong>: 4 fans at 3W each = 12W</p></li>
<!--/wp:list-item-->

<!--wp:list-item-->
<li><p><strong>Other Peripherals</strong>: 20W</p></li></ul><ul class="wp-block-list">
<!--/wp:list-item--></ul>
<!--/wp:list-->

<!--wp:paragraph-->
<p><strong>Total about = 600W. We need to have a PSU that largely overkills this wattage we calculated estimately.&nbsp;</strong></p>
<!--/wp:paragraph-->

<!--wp:paragraph-->
<p>To ensure system stability and account for potential overclocking or additional components that can be added later, it’s recommended to add <strong>20-30% headroom</strong> to your total wattage. In this case:</p>
<!--/wp:paragraph-->

<!--wp:list-->
<ul class="wp-block-list"><!--wp:list-item-->
<li><p><strong>600W x 1.25 (25% headroom)</strong> = <strong>750W</strong></p></li>
<!--/wp:list-item--></ul>
<!--/wp:list-->

<!--wp:paragraph-->
<p>So, a <strong>750W PSU</strong> would be ideal for this build including extra %25 headroom. If you plan to upgrade to a more power-hungry GPU or overclock components in the future, you might want to have a larger overkill, consider a <strong>850W or 1000W PSU for future upgrades.. </strong>As we know overclocking Cpu, Gpu and Ram may increase voltage for that component may increase its TDP (Power Consumption) and generate more heat...&nbsp;</p>
<!--/wp:paragraph-->

<!--wp:heading {"level":3}-->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">✅ 4. It’s Not Noisy or Overheating</h3>
<!--/wp:heading-->

<!--wp:paragraph-->
<p>If you notice high fan speed coming from your PSU fan during benchmark or heavy game load, this means that your PSU is heating too much, means that the PSU is inneficient for that system...&nbsp;</p>
<!--/wp:paragraph-->

<!--wp:heading-->
<h2 class="wp-block-heading">⚠️ Why You Should Not Use an Old PSU?&nbsp;</h2>
<!--/wp:heading-->

<!--wp:heading {"level":3}-->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">❌ 1. Aging Capacitors</h3>
<!--/wp:heading-->

<!--wp:paragraph-->
<p>Even if it turns on and works fine, older PSUs wear out over time. Inside the power supply unit, capacitors do wear out by time and heat, voltage regulation becomes unstable, and you could end up damaging your shiny new GPU or motherboard. You can tear out your PSU and inspect the blown/worn out capacitors inside...</p>
<!--/wp:paragraph-->

<!--wp:table {"className":"tr-caption-container"}-->
<figure class="wp-block-table tr-caption-container"><table class="has-fixed-layout"><tbody><tr><td><table align="center" cellpadding="0" cellspacing="0" class="tr-caption-container" style="margin-left: auto; margin-right: auto;"><tbody><tr><td style="text-align: center;"><a href="https://i.redd.it/axsy7r61v6771.jpg" style="margin-left: auto; margin-right: auto;"><img border="0" height="365" src="https://i.redd.it/axsy7r61v6771.jpg" width="650" /></a></td></tr><tr><td class="tr-caption" style="text-align: center;">Blown Up Capacitors inside an old PSU&nbsp;</td></tr></tbody></table></td></tr><tr><td><br /></td></tr></tbody></table></figure>
<!--/wp:table-->

<!--wp:heading {"level":3}-->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">❌ 2. Efficiency Loss</h3>
<!--/wp:heading-->

<!--wp:paragraph-->
<p>Older PSUs may be do not have 80 Plus certification or may only can be efficient rated of "Bronze". Newer Gold or Platinum rated PSUs are more efficient, saving you money in long term.</p>
<!--/wp:paragraph-->

<!--wp:heading {"level":3}-->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">❌ 3. Old ATX Versions</h3>
<!--/wp:heading-->

<!--wp:html-->
<div>If your current Build doesnt match the ATX version of your psu, you may need to get a Brand New Eligible PSU.&nbsp;</div>
<!--/wp:html-->

<!--wp:html-->
<div></div>
<!--/wp:html-->

<!--wp:html-->
<div>Currently, ATX 2.4 Might be eligible for most of the systems now. But for Newest High end Pc's, you might need ATX 3.0 PSU... ATX 3.0 Provides <strong>12VHPWR Connector </strong>which Handles up to 600W on a single cable, perfect for next-gen GPUs that demand more power. For Example, RTX 4000 Series and Radeon Rx 7000 Series Gpu's require ATX 3.0 PSU...</div>
<!--/wp:html-->

<!--wp:heading {"level":3}-->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">❌ 4. Missing Safety Features</h3>
<!--/wp:heading-->

<!--wp:paragraph-->
<p>Modern PSU's do have better protection systems than older ones. And dont forget that manufacturers are still trying to develop beter PSU's overtime passing...&nbsp;</p><p>OVP (Over Voltage Protection), OCP (Over Current Protection), SCP (Short Circuit Protection), etc are the protection systems that avoids your PSU to blow up itself or the system...</p>
<!--/wp:paragraph-->

<!--wp:paragraph-->
<p>Over heavy loads may make your PSU and its inner components to overheat. Serious meltings can cause shortcut. Make sure your PSU has OTP protection that self monitors the heat inside the PSU, shutsdown the whole system in case of overheat, that prevents the meltdown and the shortcut of the components of the PSU. Thermal protection may do the best job for protecting the system and PSU itself...&nbsp;</p>
<!--/wp:paragraph-->

<!--wp:heading {"level":3}-->
<h3 class="wp-block-heading">❌ 5. Warranty &amp; Support</h3>
<!--/wp:heading-->

<!--wp:paragraph-->
<p>Remember that a new PC Build should have new fresh warranties for each part, especially the PSU which gives power to all parts... If you use an old PSU, you might know that you will get no any suppot and backup from the manufacturer...&nbsp;</p>
