Fake AirPods used to be easy to recognise. A plastic case, a generic Bluetooth connection, a price that was obviously too low. That is no longer true. Some replicas now open with an AirPods-style animation on an iPhone, show accurate battery percentages, sit in a MagSafe case, and sound genuinely pleasant for the first few weeks.

I know because I bought a pair.

I paid for a set sold in Turkey as "Fubar AirPods 3", used them for about five months, and wrote this article to explain what I found. I am not recommending any replica model here, and I am not claiming that every counterfeit pair is terrible. I want to explain how these products work, why some of them are so convincing, where they actually fail, and why I would still pick an honest budget TWS pair instead.

## What "fake AirPods" really means

Counterfeit AirPods are wireless earbuds built to imitate Apple's product: the shape, the case, the packaging, the product name, and often parts of the software experience. Online and in shops you will see them described with a rotating set of phrases:

- "1:1 replica" or "1:1 copy"
- "OEM AirPods"
- "high copy" or "HQ AirPods"
- "clone AirPods"
- "AirPods alternative"

These labels are marketing language, not specifications. They usually say nothing about the chipset, the drivers, the battery cells, or whether the advertised ANC exists at all, and two listings can use the same phrase to describe completely different hardware.

It is worth separating two things that get mixed together in these listings:

- **TWS earbuds** are simply true wireless stereo earbuds: two independent buds with no cable between them. This is a product category, not a copy of anything.
- **Counterfeit AirPods** copy Apple's branding and identity in a way that can lead a buyer to believe the product was made or authorised by Apple.

The distinction matters because there are many legitimate, affordable TWS earbuds that look roughly like AirPods without pretending to be Apple hardware. And there are replicas that are not counterfeits in the strict legal sense, because they are sold under their own brand name while copying the design and the experience.

### When the replica wears a different brand

Some of these products are sold under names such as Subzero, WhitePods or ColorPods, with their own packaging and their own warranty claims, while the shape, the case and the marketing still point straight at AirPods.

![Subzero Air 3 wireless earbuds, a branded replica sold under its own name](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgcZX-426ZU4epHqIzOclzL3wl2PlYMQJyerHrvQXU8-yCB2jlu94_OiNe3wwDFWYfAAswpHcOac1L5kNc-7-vb0q_R97pBPT5vVJ9GjdyQSHanuqV3hy60qUGqYKf92yisthJMFd03QqG5Ng5tYUdnsAXFw7vQvsdG-mYK2J7Lp4zWxEdZ21vcUjzyNSY/s1600/image.png)

A product carrying another company's brand is not automatically a counterfeit Apple product, and a similar design is not a crime: JBL, Sony, Samsung and OnePlus all sell earbuds with comparable shapes. The problem is transparency. Who manufactures the earbuds? Which chipset and Bluetooth platform do they use? Are the battery capacity, the noise cancellation and the water-resistance numbers independently measured? Does the company ship firmware updates and honour a warranty? A logo printed on a charging case answers none of these questions.

Why are they so popular? Because genuine AirPods are expensive, and a replica promises a similar-looking experience for a fraction of the price. That appeal is understandable. It is also where the trouble starts, because the low price is not a discount. It is a different product.

## Why some replicas are so convincing: chipsets and firmware

A common assumption is that every replica uses the same cheap Bluetooth chip. That is not true. The replica market has different tiers, and the more expensive ones are built on chipset families that also appear in mainstream audio products: Airoha, Huilian, BES, and at the entry level Jieli (JL) and Bluetrum.

Chipset names in replica communities do not always match the manufacturer's official product names, and the exact hardware can change between production batches. That is why the same model name can behave differently two months later, and why a chip name in a listing should be treated as a claim rather than a fact: unless the seller documents the board or you open the case, there is no way to check it.

### Airoha

Airoha, now part of the MediaTek Group, is the name most often attached to the more advanced replicas.

![Airoha logo, part of the MediaTek Group](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgXxcuZKR8onNxNyyMUmzdtkUnQgoKn0QIqiNcQB7LX5Itc4sMC7HNPwPj1-qqVcT4hl6onPvyPgnv3Rt-r2Tva5ltXiNf5_bk641vpoBXfCED31lS_v2MnkGWaUhO_zcCz4hfWf7Vbkwwh1QiVbD1OCXv-kRj2-7hT2NWzlN0o5bX09RqwlUd9S5ExoAw/w528-h213/image.png)

Chip designations reported in replica generations include the Airoha 1562M, 1562E, 1563E and 1571AM. Depending on the implementation, these platforms can offer solid Bluetooth stability, audio processing and power management, which is one reason some replicas feel far better than generic earbuds.

![A replica AirPods Pro built on an Airoha 1562A chipset, sold on AliExpress](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEioEhJLB6ydC4I3eEmULgGPKDSBuTBLKaz-_5UjGeja2osovB8R0EEZFSL8EtisYrBUO7BKe1bMv7tayJKsKDXnRfig7vRrwJrA66LLs6kcxTfqmU_oRD1S13NlyhdNbWlOLyLm7p1mlg4491bZtYI6yfe1PLkx04a1rdu__vP1Vh0VSwJl5pURsL1kTW0/s1600/image.png)

### Huilian

Huilian platforms, reported as the A10 and 247, are associated with the most Apple-like behaviour: pairing animations, a convincing settings screen, and multi-device switching that looks familiar on an iPhone.

![A Huilian 247-equipped replica AirPods Pro 3 sold by an online seller](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEiln-ZNtVePfRIdN8ayJafy-1c2sTlxRpNyvTZfvFS1v0mHMv7JwKme1pqBVWePCT8w1qspNxccCX8U9C6FvDYGM3GO5hnHANTgqL_9cs9dPnx-fFshAG-Wy4lBRb3Fa0hB3OAcjOwQ-0VoLJZrJklJFUGiKlyf-6XsNIDQ89Jw2np60EZg2aYatBB9cgw/w310-h306/image.png)

Resembling Apple's interface is not the same as running Apple's hardware or having access to Apple's services, and that gap shows up later in this article. It is also the tier where the marketing gets most confident: sellers of Huilian-based pairs describe "true spatial audio" and "full iOS integration" as if they were licensed features.

### BES (Bestechnic)

BES chips, reported as the 2300ZP, 2500YP and 2600 series, appear in replicas that advertise stronger audio processing and noise cancellation. A better chip makes those features possible; it does not make them well implemented.

### Jieli and Bluetrum

These are the affordable Bluetooth platforms behind the cheapest replicas and lots of no-name earbuds. They can deliver perfectly usable wireless audio. They are also the tier where listings most often promise features the hardware does not have.

![Inside an original Apple AirPods, for comparison with a replica's internals](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgvVaEkXI0fz_1IInuxdxmdQWSynyY71AZQk447tVw4l6yDD9H0-IPVMRvZGtEpoR0tLuMZOfJSDUnK_i14QE6psqiqCMfldynMX1qvhUYs9kHpZQ4YzwdlFvYuWQpbk_dj-O5VJdCxjfuFGORcEhu-R4ekxe7ytTBJ2LdQ2z-LAcu3jj3No9p_-YoMrSc/w439-h439/image.png)

As a rough map of what each family tends to mean in practice:

| Platform | Chips seen in replicas | What it usually enables | The catch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Airoha | 1562M, 1562E, 1563E, 1571AM | Stable Bluetooth, decent audio processing, FlyCC settings and updates | Good chip in a weak shell is still a weak product |
| Huilian | A10, 247 | The most convincing iPhone pairing and settings behaviour | Imitated interface, no Apple hardware or services |
| BES | 2300ZP, 2500YP, 2600 series | Stronger audio processing, ANC claims | ANC quality depends on the implementation, not the chip |
| Jieli / Bluetrum | Various entry-level parts | Basic wireless audio at the lowest price | Where most impossible feature claims come from |

None of this means that the chipset decides the product. A pair is the sum of its drivers, microphones, antenna design, battery cells, firmware and assembly quality. A good chip in a badly built earbud still gives you a badly built earbud, and a listing that names a chip is not proof that the chip is inside. For a community-maintained overview of which chipset sits in which replica generation, [AirReps](https://airreps.info/) is the reference most buyers use.

## The pairing animation trick

This is the part that convinces people. Open the case of a decent replica near an iPhone and the phone can show the AirPods pop-up: the product image, the name, the battery percentages and a Connect button. It looks exactly like the real thing.

![iPhone showing an AirPods Connect pop-up next to an AirPods charging case](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhyL0ak4JfWEz0qpl5Eza6oQCufenO2s84lC_aJTRjPLQm3j6y7kEs5SDv-eVr-6aB8hnTKlQRP9BskuUQEzDO36G17oCEC-dHRykvSxUTg3WogNaL41xjhpwfA6Tdol82EwNzgyWzTj4YHeZRtRPeG5qEbqAh207oFPQ9ICIaoshI2xC-BhTOu4o4be4Q/s1600/image.png)

![A seller's photo of replica AirPods Pro connected to an iPhone with the AirPods pairing animation](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEj7TqnZwXVG67ISUbUAICZyoxS4IoNZEOxqcLDyN8osxcgpVyWbUVlbq5iSIPxab9TfE1w-taE5AoFAv8ESZSEyY4sTkiC1L9IbiYuOy3s4VRXtCQmDmi0eDd5PxklH0rm1wLGqYv_r2H5H3xCuGFJxWjiy80sl09KFePZm4h5ur94zeDuM_kbBR3Jay-M/w412-h394/image.png)

What that animation proves is that the earbuds broadcast identification data an iPhone recognises. What it does not prove:

- that there is a genuine Apple H1 or H2 chip inside
- that the earbuds are part of Apple's Find My network
- that the device is associated with your iCloud account
- that the firmware will keep working after the next iOS update
- that you have Apple's security and authentication hardware

The same applies to MagSafe: a magnet ring and a charging coil are cheap to copy, and a replica case that snaps onto a MagSafe charger is still a replica case.

![Find My on iPhone showing an AirPods Pro card and a charging case](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgz5u_qU76BUS3xhc-5cQmFolNDBckNDbMCMM3LxtgJ6bk09ZwCzdKrVNIRljVLC7Yx7eqNlKTe0Roi-6iA1cay4oYymwDPtkJOEK9SSh4ppKEc5C0sW_jFylacHl5Oqux8A-Q4O6ZpJ0yt6D5KImw_tjPjCDWVMqc1KITFav6ENIiTi5TsNVnVod9iUUI/s1600/image.png)

Find My is the clearest example of the confusion. A replica can produce a Find My entry on a paired iPhone, which looks like Apple's tracking feature without being Apple's Find My network. A seller's video of a pairing animation is a demonstration of firmware, not of authenticity.

It helps to know what the genuine integration actually is. On real AirPods, the earbuds hand off between your iPhone, iPad and Mac, appear in Find My through Apple's network, receive firmware updates through iOS, and are covered by Apple's warranty and service programme. Every one of those depends on Apple hardware and Apple's servers, which is why no replica can reproduce them, no matter how convincing the pop-up looks.

## My five months with a "Fubar AirPods 3"

I want to include my own experience, because not every replica is a disaster on day one, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest.

I bought a pair sold as Fubar AirPods 3 with a charging case. The box carried no Apple logo, just "AirPods 3" and a mention of MagSafe support — a replica that copies Apple's styling without printing Apple's trademark on the packaging.

![My Fubar AirPods 3 box and charging case, bought online in Turkey](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEiKVMrlnRWsux29e846WwSKphKo-8JiMRU1ESnVlKnDAkRXu3ZEBkJcFYRSSEqF2LZBWgbuKPeD0k2VoS6WEDOuSBU1LAKKhnAI-BP0Wnx0J_bFPfRMQem8ODfJ87effZxleUQlwqSEnYEw9u4Zd_NUHyRgEtl9OudFMwwt9w_eORNZjhMOnxU3CJxfh94/w387-h387/image.png)

The first impression was better than I expected. Sound quality was genuinely good. The microphone was bad, but I mostly use the phone's own microphone for calls anyway. Pairing was instant: I opened the case, pressed the button, and my Samsung Galaxy S23 FE listed the earbuds as "AirPods" and connected without any trouble. On an iPhone they triggered the AirPods 3 animation.

![Samsung Galaxy S23 FE Bluetooth settings showing the replica earbuds paired as "AirPods"](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEg3Je4UHElSxD8N4xgrXymqoH8NC9IgLqoMIrtxSchx2neuAoVFVGqLJXfqCjANnw4hqbB5JEwO6cRRgI8nlUG6HzSTMKvV3gAseF_89o4OWDWyj55hZth42SZ932MvKNcw1uErcQrynU2uFmnkRbziM6XxcOdJBO0m0eaDWNpIPKsqQQWDQ4bZLsfHxv0/w229-h495/Screenshot_20261107_230707_Settings.jpg)

I could not confirm which chipset was inside. The seller provided nothing, and without opening the case and inspecting the board, calling it Airoha, Huilian or BES would be a guess. ![PodsBattery on Android showing the battery level of my replica AirPods](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEibX_qA0IjpfaMKLqwQ3n18VLmXh_ivumu0v_33Xk2Yeb8shvactnyBH4gctnoQ2wHbzMdWHwOx5h-502Y3FB-JAwKGyoVHLyLMCtYmAWwkQcSM71i54NUzQvfpv32fmd3bARjN3I4yzMd7oBR_GCfN2wx7ElJ5RS4boi67ohgKAU0hv6QiUQiYsmqWl3w/w219-h473/Screenshot_20261107_230759_PodsBattery.jpg)

For ordinary use the pair was fine. Music and video sounded good, the fit was comfortable enough for a few hours at a time, the case charged over USB-C without complaint, and the controls did what they were supposed to. Nothing about the first month suggested a product that would fail.

Because I was on Android, I monitored the battery with the PodsBattery app, which recognised the earbuds and showed both buds and the case. Android itself warned me when the battery dropped below 10%.

Then came the part no product listing mentions. After two or three months, the left earbud began losing sound. By about five months in total, the left earbud stopped connecting altogether, and the pair was effectively dead. The sound quality I liked in week one was not the product. The product was five months of use.

That is the honest summary of my own replica: a convincing first month, a decent second, and a failure that no warranty would cover.

## Where replicas fall apart in daily use

The pattern repeats across the categories below, and it comes from the same root cause: inconsistent components, no quality control, and no support once the sale is done.

| Area | What the listing promises | What you often get |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sound | Rich bass, balanced tuning | Unstable tuning, muddy midrange, distortion at higher volume, one earbud louder than the other |
| Battery | "Up to X hours" | One earbud draining faster, inaccurate percentages, a case that loses capacity quickly |
| ANC | Active noise cancellation | A passive seal with a sticker on the box, or cancellation that does nothing |
| Calls | Clear voice pickup | Muffled recordings, background noise, inconsistent volume |
| Connection | Seamless pairing | Random disconnects, one earbud failing to connect after an update |
| Longevity | A premium product | Loose contacts, worn hinges, no spare parts, no warranty once the seller disappears |

### Inconsistency is the real problem

Two pairs sold with identical descriptions can contain different drivers, different firmware and different chipsets, depending on the batch. What that looks like in practice:

- weak or uncontrolled bass, with an EQ that drifts between tracks
- a muddy midrange where voices sit behind the instruments
- treble that turns harsh and painful at higher volume
- outright distortion when you push the volume up
- almost no stereo separation, so both earbuds sound close to mono
- one earbud noticeably louder than the other, which is tiring over a long session

You cannot judge any of this from a product photo or a spec sheet, and the seller's photos are usually of a sample they hand-picked. The only useful evidence is independent measurement or a long run of buyer reports.

### Battery and charging safety

Earbuds contain small lithium-ion cells, and the case contains another. In cheap pairs the battery is usually where the corner-cutting becomes visible:

- one earbud draining noticeably faster than the other
- percentages that jump, freeze or reset
- a case that holds less charge every month
- earbuds that only charge if you reseat them in the case
- sudden shutdowns at 30 or 40 percent
- cells that swell inside a sealed case

Safe charging depends on a protection circuit, thermal control and consistent manufacturing. Cheap, uncertified cells can overheat or fail, and a counterfeit product gives you no way to check which cells you received. Buying a product with a real brand and a real certification is not about snobbery; it is the only way to know what is inside the case.

![A cheap replica AirPods 4 box with an ANC sticker added by the seller](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgC_TvOARCUImJxe9NI1aW7F5Yu82ydXWh4t574YH8mUBC7WYxuJlUMcswQU-jREBDnvwfSzeypot9Jy5IPJNyqEswrurepczeV9Mt9426RqnMNyyC3Q76p1eF5XURT6QZuWNG5gU68UYAY1NFMcY0Gq2a-S2gPtLLTrNijigwy3MTvVyGncQebB-K3SvY/s1600/image.png)

### "ANC" that is only a word

Noise cancellation is advertised heavily and implemented inconsistently. A sticker printing "100% ANC/ENC" on the box costs nothing, and in Turkey these replica AirPods 4 sets with "ANC" were selling for around 8 USD when I checked. Real cancellation needs microphones that sample the noise, processing fast enough to invert it, and someone who can measure the result. On a replica the sticker is usually the only part of that chain that exists.

The other half of the confusion is the difference between active noise cancellation and passive isolation. Passive isolation comes from the fit and the ear tips. ANC uses microphones and signal processing to reduce noise around you. A tight-sealing bud with no ANC can feel quieter than a loose bud with real ANC, which is why sellers get away with the claim.

![iPhone settings for AirPods Pro showing Transparency, Adaptive and Noise Cancellation modes](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEj3VzqbP1UKbgMAT8-G9jRL_mZxpcW6ouo1IAwPeOTybC_r9UTztPER3sBhAokdc8EsX_5vhmoWvZSc81v1xpe_cVeVTGlGInGtgssfRSc7XG4siEPIPL-uZWhAKOg65_qENmz1mMpUxSS0JKbsUXMMk4LrHcd4WkOvU6ClH6BawHXX_Y5bcr6kMp_VM24/s1600/image.png)

### Microphones, updates and support

A pair can sound acceptable for music and still fail on calls: muffled voice, background noise, uneven volume. This is hard to test from a video and easy to hide in a listing.

Connection problems are the other recurring theme:

- random disconnects when the phone is in a pocket or a bag
- one earbud failing to wake up after the case
- audio drifting out of sync with video
- touch controls that trigger on their own
- the pair vanishing from the Bluetooth list after an OS update

Software support is the quieter problem. A replica imitates today's iOS or Android behaviour. When the next release changes how Bluetooth devices are handled, there is nobody to ship a fix. The same applies to hardware: loose charging contacts, weak hinges and dead batteries become unfixable, because there are no spare parts and no service centre to send them to.

## When the case has a touchscreen

One of the stranger developments is the replica charging case with a built-in display. MacRumors reported on these in March 2024, noting that the design follows a 2021 Apple patent that Apple never shipped as a product, and later reviewed one of the units directly.

![A replica AirPods Pro 2 case with a built-in touchscreen display](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEixxgLWztJ5h7QpQchU5ixDiOU4E9N943o7OEGMZKJ76zZxtEqlAgPAnFxSgU8L8de6r47-fCcb2wam50UL54IvgJrM5nxnzTlRtFy9O5FQgff4IcyUh-QMHW1vw3zR_ffM6gTGJltxopKDAuLszZdU-Jk3RObauUVWNwPyWuIXeQ3ZnRBBrpy3QoP8Nf0/s1600/image.png)

The idea is more interesting than the execution. Depending on the model, the case display can show:

- battery for the left and right earbud
- the case's own percentage
- Bluetooth status and the time
- noise control and transparency modes
- equaliser presets
- wallpapers and other cosmetic themes
- on some versions, a Find My-style screen and a camera shutter button In MacRumors' own [hands-on review](https://www.macrumors.com/2024/04/05/fake-airpods-with-display/), the touchscreen was unreliable, several buttons did nothing, the sound was mediocre, and the equaliser presets made things worse. A screen is an extra component and a new failure point, not a quality signal.

## Controlling replicas on Android

Genuine AirPods are limited on Android, and replicas fill that gap with companion apps. Compatibility depends entirely on the chipset and the firmware, so no app works with every pair.

**PodsBattery** ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.yugongkeji.podstool)) is the app I used. It provides:

- a battery pop-up when compatible earbuds connect
- separate left and right levels, plus the case on supported models
- battery notifications in the status bar
- home-screen widgets and a Dynamic Island-style display
- in-ear detection and a few touch-control options on supported hardware

Its developer explicitly lists clone AirPods among the supported devices, which is why it works with pairs Apple's own software ignores. It is still only a monitor: there is no equaliser and no ANC control, and some clones report their levels in a way the app misreads.

![PodsBattery battery pop-up interface for AirPods on Android](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEgxwIHF4UcfPh6FLO9c5XsonuzuImQ6X9yjTkrs823wytZ0XONXFxNRD_skOoebSuN8PEr0kpGKEs14gZXMmH76IFdqZu3vWdLemFZsxDbXutclbNfgyepjeM50chBKvJou_OsaGeNOSFvC0n0BRCI768UVt7C6QwmtJkdohPsHhX95EckfYk8YCDRQpYU/w282-h502/image.png)

**AndroPods** ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=pro.vitalii.andropods)) works the other way round: it makes any AirPods-style pair behave more like AirPods on Android, adding connection pop-ups, battery notifications, play/pause on removing an earbud and a voice-assistant shortcut. It asks for location access, which Android requires for Bluetooth LE discovery, and it works around Android's limitations rather than unlocking anything in the earbuds.

![The AndroPods companion app for Android](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEirhCuxckyyDBxTMO8lgLqRJQ_nX642jSPw4o_BcVXx41VoZb6tytCDG1X7Uz7WD4yO9sceaRBGJcIFdm0w37s-BQR87fomDtQXethY-No9ciOMvJwT9YXsqA5bmL_iVWc7ViBrIRr3mrLGPEs8ora7C13pYXLM4MP87-VB74kz2bmtNQbWme30WZ62_oE/w263-h554/image.png)

Chipset tools go deeper, and they are the ones to handle carefully. **FlyCC**, for TigerBuilder and Airoha-based platforms, can expose EQ profiles, touch-control settings, ANC modes and firmware updates on compatible hardware. **StarFun** does the equivalent for Huilian, **Behool** for BES and **BullSuper** for HR. The catch is that these apps are mostly distributed as APKs outside the Play Store, each one only detects its own chipset, and a firmware package for the wrong revision can leave the earbuds unable to connect at all.

![Samsung's built-in equaliser settings on Android](https://blogger.googleusercontent.com/img/b/R29vZ2xl/AVvXsEhk0GU9oAlALxzE-wLrZedzxNu2PaUzNX5EGuvcGHLMKoxMqy8zSYFa4ssT9N5yAVFJc7hyj3UCg6wSTmdnX1TlzlST4InydoKhZh25T66_Jinj9Y1cKR9OC9A7rdHKKLed_G_LgGNZo0pyO4UEp6UKiRAltKQNtYdQTqZT2TeUg5gET8Kf70neF1D0QVc/s1600/image.png)

If all you want is a different tonal balance, Android's own equaliser or a normal music app will do it. No software setting adds ANC to earbuds that lack the microphones and processing for it.

## Why a legitimate budget pair wins

I am not arguing that everyone should buy expensive earbuds. Budget TWS products make sense for most people, and several brands sell genuinely good ones for the price of a mid-range replica.

What you get from a real brand, even a cheap one:

- a manufacturer you can identify, with an address and a support channel
- published specifications instead of marketing adjectives
- a warranty and a return process in your country
- independent reviews and measurements from people who bought the product
- firmware updates when something breaks
- spare parts and a resale value

Examples of the tier I mean, all of which publish their specs and have service networks: the [Anker soundcore P20i](https://www.soundcore.com/products/p20i), the [QCY MeloBuds N60](https://www.qcy.com/products/qcy-melobuds-n60), Havit's [TWS range](https://www.havit.hk/), or JBL's Vibe series. None of them pretends to be an AirPod. All of them will tell you what you are buying.

Prices move constantly, so check the local price rather than a US listing. In Turkey, a legitimate budget pair and a "good" replica often land in the same bracket, and only one of those comes with a receipt and a warranty.

A short checklist before you pay:

- Can you name the manufacturer and find its support page?
- Does the listing publish battery capacity, driver size and Bluetooth version as numbers?
- Is there a warranty that is valid in your country, not just "seller warranty"?
- Do independent reviews exist for this exact model?
- Does the company ship firmware updates?

If the answer to the first question is no, the rest do not matter.

## So, are fake AirPods worth buying in 2026?

No, and the reason is not that they sound bad. Some of them sound fine. The reason is that you cannot know what you are buying, and you have no recourse when it fails.

The price picture makes the point. In Turkey, replica AirPods sell for anything from roughly 350 to 2,000 Turkish lira depending on how convincing the copy is, with the acceptable-looking ones around 10 to 13 USD. That is cheap enough to treat as a disposable gadget, and many people do. In the US and the EU, the same replicas cost considerably more, which removes the only argument they had. At that point you are paying real money for an imitation with no support.

There is also the branding question. You do not need your earbuds to pretend they are AirPods. You need them to sound decent, stay connected, hold a charge and be replaced if they fail.

## If you already own a replica

Plenty of people will read this after buying one, so a few practical notes:

- **Do not rely on it for anything important.** Calls with clients, interviews and online exams are exactly where a failing microphone or a dead earbud hurts most. Keep a wired pair or the phone's own microphone as a backup.
- **Be careful with companion APKs.** Install them only from the developer's own page and check the permissions they ask for. A battery monitor does not need access to your contacts or your files.
- **Do not flash firmware you are not sure about.** A wrong package can leave the earbuds undetectable, and there is no recovery process and no service centre.
- **Watch the battery.** If a case or an earbud gets hot while charging, swells, or smells of solvent, stop using it. Do not puncture or bin a swollen cell; take it to an electronics recycling point.
- **Keep the receipt if you bought from a shop.** A branded replica sold with a local warranty is at least a product with a seller behind it, and consumer protection rules may apply to it.

## Final thoughts

My own pair is the summary of this article. It was convincing, it sounded good, it paired like the real thing, and it was dead inside five months with nothing to fall back on. That first impression is exactly what the product is designed to sell you.

If you want AirPods, save for AirPods. If you want good wireless earbuds for less, buy them from a company that will still exist when something goes wrong. Good value is a product that keeps working, not a product that looks expensive for a low price.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are fake AirPods worth buying?

I would not recommend them. Some replicas sound surprisingly good, but inconsistent manufacturing, unreliable batteries, no warranty and no long-term support make them a poor purchase, especially once they cost more than an honest budget pair.

### Are replica AirPods the same as original AirPods?

No. They are not Apple products. Similar designs, pairing animations and battery indicators do not mean the earbuds contain Apple hardware or have access to Apple's services.

### Why do fake AirPods show the pairing animation on an iPhone?

Some replicas broadcast identification data that iOS recognises, so the phone displays the familiar AirPods pop-up. The animation shows that the firmware imitates Apple's protocol; it does not show that there is an Apple chip inside.

### Which chipsets are used in fake AirPods?

Different generations use different platforms: Airoha, Huilian, BES, and at the cheap end Jieli and Bluetrum. Airoha-based pairs tend to have the most reliable Bluetooth and are supported by the FlyCC app, Huilian-based pairs imitate the iPhone experience most closely, and BES-based pairs are the ones that advertise stronger noise cancellation. The actual chip depends on the model and the production batch, and sellers rarely document it.

### Does an Airoha or Huilian chip make them genuine Apple products?

No. These are independent Bluetooth audio platforms made by other companies. Their presence says something about the replica's capabilities, nothing about its authenticity.

### Can fake AirPods have a touchscreen charging case?

Yes. Some modified replicas put a small display in the case for battery levels, noise control and equaliser presets. It is an extra part that can fail, not evidence of quality.

### Can I control fake AirPods on Android?

Partially. Apps such as PodsBattery and AndroPods add battery monitoring and connection pop-ups, while chipset tools such as FlyCC or StarFun expose deeper settings on supported hardware. Compatibility varies by chip and firmware.

### Can I adjust EQ or ANC on Android?

Some compatible replicas allow EQ and ANC adjustments through chipset-specific apps. Software cannot add genuine noise cancellation to earbuds that lack the microphones and processing for it.

### Are cheap TWS earbuds better than fake AirPods?

For most people, yes. A legitimate budget pair has an identifiable manufacturer, published specifications, a warranty and independent reviews, which is a much better basis for a purchase than an imitation.

### Are fake AirPods safe? Can they damage my phone?

There is no reason to assume every replica damages a phone, but uncertified batteries and unknown charging circuits are a real risk, and sideloaded companion apps should be treated with caution. A product from an identifiable manufacturer avoids both problems.

### Why are fake AirPods so cheap?

Cheaper components, simpler manufacturing, no quality control, no warranty, no support and no certification. You are not getting a discount on an AirPod; you are buying a different product that looks like one.
